My early concept is a text-based 'day in the life' simulator which the user interacts in first-person as a character. I think of it as an anxiety simulator, with each option from launch causing a worsening or stagnation of the narrator's condition. I have hopes to make it, although with limited choices, quite expansive, allowing many different experiences, all threaded through with a Groundhog day theme -- the user is fustratingly unable to escape the  self-depricating, pesssimistic and overly-anxious 'self'/narrator they must play as. The idea is that no matter what rabbit hole/experience they go down, all will end with the idea they can 'try again tomorrow' to have a good day, thus looping to the beginning, with no real result of being able to 'escape' this hell. The user will have options, but all of them will be limited to poor results. I have done some mapping of the first section, in which the user wakes up and chooses things to do to start the day. Their choices will lead them to either end up choosing to leave the house, or stay home. Leaving the house will lead to different sections such as going to the store or the park, and staying home will cause them to doomscroll on their laptop. Some of the choices they can go down will mean their day ends early, with the most long path causing them to go to a party. Left to change/ergotics is their decisions, and what they are tied by is that there is no satisfying finish to the game - leaning into the knowsn frustration of lack of choice. I want to leave the user with such frustration that strangely enough, they hate it and don't want to play, because of an assumed presuption they can somehow 'win', and the realisation that they can't.
